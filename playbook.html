The Setup — Bow & Arrow Pullback
1Identify a stock in a hot sector showing relative strength to its peers and the broader market.
2Stock must have a history of momentum when it has broken out previously. Character matters — if it hasn't moved before, why now?
3Wait for the stock to pull back into its rising 21 EMA or 50 SMA. The longer and tighter the consolidation, the more energy built up — rubber band effect.
4Confirm the bounce. Do not enter on the way down. Wait for price to take out the prior day's high or show a strong reclaim candle with volume.
5Check market environment first: Are breakouts working? Is risk appetite on? Is the sector ETF above its key MAs? If environment is murky — sit on hands.
Entry Rules
1Enter on the first candle that takes out the prior day's high after price has touched or come within 3% of the 21 EMA or 50 SMA.
2Starter size first — add to full size only after the breakout level holds and acts well. Never be too big in the middle of the range.
3Grade the trade before entering: A+ = hot sector + relative strength + clean base + multiple timeframe confluence. B = one or more checks missing — size down.
4Both 21 EMA and 50 SMA matter. The 21 EMA is your short-term trend anchor. The 50 SMA is the intermediate. When both converge — highest conviction.
Exit & Stop Rules
1Stop = below the consolidation low or below the MA that triggered the entry. This is the TRUE stop. Do not tighten it intraday — you will get shaken out of good trades.
2Do not sell breakouts on day one of an A+ trade. Let it run. Trim only when extended 1+ ATR from entry.
3Once follow-through confirmed, trail against prior day's low for mid/large caps. Multi-day VWAP from breakout day for small caps.
4Last in, first out: if the market has been running 3–4 weeks, new buys get a tighter leash. If it doesn't work quickly — cut it. Don't let new buys become baggage.
5A bad close below the MA on a closing basis = reduce or exit. Do not hold hoping for morning recovery.
Market Environment Checks — Run These Before Any Trade
✓Breadth: Are we in a broad bull trend or just a few names carrying? Breadth matters most at major turns — not in the middle of a trend.
✓Risk Appetite: Are offensive sectors (tech, semis, small caps) leading? Or is it utilities and healthcare? Crypto as a barometer — if Bitcoin is failing breakouts, caution.
✓Market Behavior: Are breakouts following through? Is good news being bought or sold? This is the most important check. If the last 5 breakouts failed — do not buy the 6th.
